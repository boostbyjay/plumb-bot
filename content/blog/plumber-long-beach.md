Long Beach is LA County's second-largest city, and its plumbing needs are as diverse as its neighborhoods. From the beach communities of Belmont Shore and Naples Island to the historic bungalows of the Alamitos Beach area, from the suburban family neighborhoods of Bixby Knolls and Los Altos to the downtown lofts and commercial corridors — Long Beach has every type of plumbing challenge you can imagine.

## Long Beach by neighborhood

**Belmont Shore and Naples Island.** These coastal communities face salt air corrosion on exterior plumbing, higher water tables, and a concentration of vacation rentals that stress plumbing systems. Outdoor faucets, pool equipment, and irrigation systems need extra attention.

**Downtown and waterfront.** Downtown Long Beach lofts and condos have commercial-grade plumbing systems that need different expertise than single-family homes. Older buildings may have shared plumbing infrastructure that requires building-wide coordination.

**Bixby Knolls and Los Altos.** These established residential neighborhoods have many homes built in the 1920s through 1960s. Original plumbing, aging water heaters, and clay sewer lines are common. These are the areas where whole-home repiping projects come up most frequently.

**The Peninsula and San Pedro area.** Closer to the Port of Long Beach, these areas have older infrastructure and a working-class housing stock that needs honest, affordable plumbing services.

## What all Long Beach homes have in common

Hard water. The Long Beach water supply, like most of LA and Orange County, carries high mineral content. Water heaters work harder and die younger. Fixtures get clogged with scale. Annual water heater flushing is essential.
