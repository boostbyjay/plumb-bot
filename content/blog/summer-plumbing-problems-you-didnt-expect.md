Winter gets all the attention when it comes to plumbing worries, thanks to frozen pipes and the fear of a burst line. But summer brings its own lineup of plumbing stress, mostly tied to how differently we use water and our homes during the warmer months. Here's what tends to catch homeowners off guard.

## 1. Sprinkler systems under constant strain

Summer means daily or near-daily irrigation, which is a huge jump from the occasional watering most systems see the rest of the year. That extra use exposes weaknesses fast: valves that were fine running occasionally can start sticking, cracked heads that went unnoticed in spring become obvious leaks, and pressure issues that only show up under heavier, more frequent demand.

## 2. Septic systems getting overloaded

If you have guests staying over, kids home from school, or just generally more people showering and doing laundry during the summer, your septic system is handling a noticeably higher volume of wastewater than it does the rest of the year. Overloading a septic system, especially over a short period like a holiday weekend with visitors, can push it past its capacity to properly treat wastewater before it reaches the drain field.

## 3. Garbage disposal overuse from grilling and produce season

Summer cooking tends to generate more food scraps: corn husks, watermelon rinds, peach pits, and fibrous vegetable trimmings from all that fresh produce. Many of these items are exactly the kind of fibrous or hard material that jams a garbage disposal or wraps around the blades instead of grinding cleanly. If you've noticed your disposal working harder or jamming more often in summer, this is usually why.

## 4. Increased water heater demand from more showers

More outdoor activity, yard work, and swimming often means more showers per day per household, especially with kids. A water heater that handles a typical winter routine just fine can feel strained when demand jumps, sometimes revealing sediment buildup or an aging heating element that wasn't obvious before.

## 5. Vacation leak risks and outdoor faucet wear

Summer is peak vacation season, and an empty house is exactly when a small leak has the most time and opportunity to do serious damage. A slow drip from a supply line or a failing water heater connection that would normally get noticed within a day can run unchecked for a week or two while you're away, sometimes resulting in significant water damage by the time you return.

Hoses left connected and pressurized all summer, combined with more frequent use for washing cars, filling pools, and watering plants, also put real wear on outdoor faucets and their connections. Watch for slow leaks at the connection point, which waste water and can sometimes indicate a worn washer inside the faucet itself.

## A few simple precautions

- Walk your sprinkler zones periodically to catch broken heads or leaks early
- Spread out laundry and heavy water use if you have a septic system and extra guests
- Keep fibrous and hard food scraps out of the disposal, even in the middle of grilling season
- Before a longer trip, shut off the main water valve or at least check for any slow leaks beforehand
- Disconnect hoses when not in use to reduce strain on outdoor faucets
