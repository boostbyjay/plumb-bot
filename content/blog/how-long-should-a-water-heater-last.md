"How old is too old?" is one of the most common questions homeowners ask about their water heater, usually right after it starts making a weird noise or the hot water runs out faster than it used to. Here's a straightforward answer, along with the factors that actually move that number up or down.

## Average lifespan by type

- **Standard tank water heaters:** typically 8 to 12 years
- **Tankless water heaters:** typically 15 to 20 years with proper maintenance

These ranges assume reasonably normal water conditions and at least occasional maintenance. On the low end or high end of these ranges, the difference usually comes down to a few specific factors.

## What shortens a water heater's life

**Hard water.** Homes with hard water see faster mineral and sediment buildup inside the tank, which insulates the water from the heating element or burner and makes the whole system work harder. Over years, this buildup contributes to earlier tank failure and is one of the most common reasons a water heater doesn't make it to the average lifespan.

**No maintenance.** A tank that's never been flushed accumulates sediment continuously. Combined with hard water, this can shave years off a tank's functional life. On the flip side, an annual flush (see our guide on flushing a water heater) is one of the simplest ways to push a tank toward the higher end of its expected range.

**A worn anode rod.** The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod inside the tank designed to corrode instead of the tank itself, a process called cathodic protection. Once the anode rod is fully consumed, usually after several years, the tank's interior lining becomes the next thing corrosion attacks. Replacing the anode rod periodically, something a plumber can check during routine service, can meaningfully extend tank life.

**High water pressure or temperature settings.** Consistently high household water pressure or a thermostat set well above the recommended 120°F both add stress to internal components over time.

## How to find your water heater's actual age

Water heaters don't usually list a plain manufacture date, but the serial number on the manufacturer's label almost always encodes it. The exact format varies by brand, but a common pattern uses the first two digits or first letter-and-number combination to represent the year and week of manufacture. If you're not sure how to decode your specific brand's serial number, a quick search for "[your brand] water heater serial number date" usually turns up a clear answer, or the manufacturer's customer service line can tell you directly.

## Planning around age, not just symptoms

If your tank is within its expected lifespan and showing no symptoms, there's no need to replace it preemptively. But if it's approaching or past the top of its expected range, it's worth planning a replacement on your own schedule rather than waiting for a failure that could mean a flooded closet or garage.
