The Palos Verdes Peninsula — encompassing Palos Verdes Estates, Rolling Hills, and Rolling Hills Estates — is one of the most exclusive residential areas in Southern California. Ocean views, large lots, custom-built homes, and a commitment to preserving the area's natural beauty create a set of plumbing needs that are different from anywhere else in LA County.

## Peninsula plumbing challenges

**Salt air corrosion.** Coastal proximity means accelerated corrosion on exterior plumbing fixtures, outdoor faucets, pool equipment, and any exposed copper or brass components. Peninsula homes need more frequent inspection of outdoor plumbing and corrosion-resistant materials for new installations.

**Large custom homes.** Many PV properties are custom-built on large lots with complex plumbing systems — multiple bathrooms, guest houses, pool and spa systems, outdoor kitchens, and irrigation. These systems need a plumber with experience in high-end residential plumbing.

**Mature tree root systems.** The Peninsula's extensive mature landscaping includes oaks, eucalyptus, and palms with aggressive root systems. Clay and cast iron sewer lines are vulnerable to root intrusion, and the large lot sizes mean sewer runs are often longer and more complex than in standard subdivisions.

**Seismic considerations.** The Palos Verdes Peninsula sits on a hillside above the ocean, and the area is subject to ground movement and soil shifting. Pipe joints and connections can shift over time, and earthquake retrofitting of water heaters and gas lines is an important safety consideration.

**Water pressure on hillside properties.** Homes built on the Peninsula's hillsides often face water pressure challenges. Gravity-fed systems from lower-elevation water mains may not provide adequate pressure for upper floors and distant fixtures. Pressure-boosting systems are sometimes necessary.
