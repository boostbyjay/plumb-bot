Santa Monica is one of the most desirable addresses on the Westside — ocean views, pier access, and a walkable downtown that draws visitors from around the world. But the same coastal location that makes Santa Monica special also creates specific plumbing challenges that inland homeowners never face.

## Santa Monica's coastal plumbing challenges

**Salt air corrosion.** This is the defining challenge for Santa Monica plumbing. Homes near the beach — and even those a few blocks inland — experience accelerated corrosion on exterior plumbing fixtures, outdoor faucets, copper piping in exterior walls, and pool equipment. Salt in the air attacks metal components, shortening their lifespan significantly compared to inland homes.

**Aging housing stock near the beach.** The area between the beach and Wilshire Boulevard has many homes built in the 1940s through 1960s. Original galvanized pipes, aging water heaters, and clay sewer lines are common. These homes need regular plumbing maintenance and eventual system upgrades.

**Vacation rental pressure.** Santa Monica has a high concentration of short-term rentals and vacation properties. High turnover means plumbing systems experience heavier-than-normal use, and deferred maintenance between guests leads to more emergency calls.

**Hard water from the LA supply.** Despite being coastal, Santa Monica gets its water from LA's municipal supply, which carries significant mineral content. Water heaters battle sediment, and scale builds up on fixtures.

## Tips for Santa Monica homeowners

- Inspect exterior plumbing annually for corrosion signs
- Flush your water heater every 6 months (hard water + salt air = extra stress)
- Replace outdoor faucet washers and check for leaks regularly
- Schedule sewer camera inspections for older homes
