A burst or leaking pipe rarely happens on a convenient schedule. It's usually a weekend, a holiday, or the middle of the night, exactly when getting a professional out immediately is hardest. Having a few inexpensive supplies stocked ahead of time won't fix the underlying problem, but it can buy you real time and prevent a small leak from turning into serious water damage while you wait for help.

## What to keep on hand

- **Pipe repair clamps.** These metal clamps with a rubber gasket wrap directly around a leaking section of pipe and cinch down to stop or slow a leak. Keep a couple of different sizes since pipe diameters vary throughout a home.
- **Self-fusing silicone tape.** Unlike ordinary electrical or duct tape, this type of tape bonds to itself when wrapped tightly, creating a flexible, watertight seal around a small crack or pinhole leak. It stretches significantly, which helps it conform to fittings and joints.
- **Epoxy putty.** A two-part putty that you knead together and press directly onto a leak or crack. It hardens as it cures, useful for small holes or cracks in a pipe, though it needs a relatively dry surface to bond well.
- **A large bucket or two.** Simple, but essential for catching water while you shut off the supply and apply a temporary fix, or just to contain drips until a plumber arrives.
- **Old towels or rags.** For soaking up what's already spilled and protecting flooring or cabinetry from further water exposure.
- **A flashlight or headlamp.** Pipe leaks often happen in dark, cramped spaces like under sinks or in crawlspaces, and you'll want both hands free to work.

## Where to keep this kit

Store everything together in a labeled bin or bag somewhere easy to grab quickly, like a utility closet or garage shelf, rather than scattered across different drawers. In an actual emergency, you don't want to be hunting for supplies while water is actively spreading.

## A few extras worth adding

- **Adjustable wrench.** Useful for shutting off supply valves that have corroded slightly and don't turn easily by hand.
- **Duct tape and a marker.** Handy for labeling shutoff valves ahead of time so you (or anyone else home during an emergency) can find the right one quickly without guessing.
- **A small flashlight-friendly notebook or your phone.** Jotting down when a leak started and what you tried can help a plumber diagnose the issue faster when they arrive.

Building this kit doesn't take an afternoon. Most of these items are inexpensive and available at any hardware store, and assembling it now means you're not making a panicked trip to buy supplies while a pipe is actively leaking.

## An important reminder about these tools

Every item on this list is designed to buy time, not to permanently solve the problem. A pipe repair clamp or a wrap of silicone tape can hold back a leak for hours or sometimes days, which is often exactly what you need to safely wait for a plumber rather than dealing with an emergency in the middle of the night. But none of these are a substitute for identifying and properly repairing whatever caused the pipe to fail in the first place, whether that's corrosion, a bad joint, or freeze damage.

Using these tools also assumes you've already shut off the water supply to the affected area or the whole house; none of them are meant to be applied to a pipe that's still under full pressure.
