Mold around a bathtub gets treated like a cleaning problem more often than it should. Sometimes that's exactly what it is. Other times, the dark spots along the caulk line are the visible tip of a leak that's been quietly working behind your tile for a while. Telling the two apart matters, because one needs a scrub brush and the other needs a repair.

## Surface mildew: the common, harmless kind

Bathrooms are humid by nature, and mildew (a milder cousin of mold) thrives wherever moisture lingers on a surface without drying out fully. This type typically shows up as:

- A thin, black or pinkish film along the caulk line or grout
- Spots that wipe away fairly easily with a mold-specific cleaner
- Growth concentrated where air circulation is weakest, like corners and the ceiling above the tub
- No accompanying softness, staining, or odor beyond the bathroom itself

If your bathroom mold matches this description, the fix has more to do with ventilation than plumbing. Run the exhaust fan during and for twenty minutes after every shower, wipe down wet surfaces, and consider re-caulking if the existing caulk has thinned or pulled away from the tile.

## The kind that means a real leak

Mold caused by an actual water leak behaves differently, and it's worth learning the difference before you spend another weekend scrubbing something that keeps coming back.

**It keeps returning no matter how well you clean it.** If mold reappears within days of a thorough cleaning, especially in the same spot, something is continuing to feed it with moisture from behind the surface, not just from a humid shower.

**It shows up in places surface humidity shouldn't reach.** Mold appearing on the wall outside the tub, on the floor near the tub's base, or on the ceiling of the room below points to water getting somewhere it isn't supposed to be.

**The wall or floor nearby feels soft.** Press gently on drywall or flooring near the tub. Any give or sponginess is a strong sign that water has been soaking into material behind the visible surface.

**There's a musty smell that lingers.** A persistent, damp odor that doesn't clear with normal cleaning and ventilation often means moisture trapped somewhere the fan can't reach.

## What usually causes the leak

The most frequent culprit is failed caulk or grout around the tub or shower that's let water seep behind the tile with every use. Over months, that moisture saturates the wall material and creates ideal mold conditions in a spot you can't see or easily clean. A cracked shower pan liner underneath a tile floor can cause the same problem from below instead of the side.

## What to do next

If you're dealing with straightforward surface mildew, better ventilation and a caulk refresh will usually solve it for good. But if mold keeps coming back, shows up in unexpected spots, or comes with soft walls, discoloration, or a persistent musty smell, cleaning the surface won't fix the underlying issue, it'll just buy you a few weeks before it's back.
