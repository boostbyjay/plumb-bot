A slow-draining bathtub is almost always a hair problem, and reaching for a bottle of chemical drain cleaner might seem like the fastest fix. It isn't always the safest one, though, especially if your home has older pipes that can be damaged by repeated exposure to caustic chemicals. The good news is that a hands-on approach works just as well, without the fumes or the risk.

## Tools you'll need

- A screwdriver (Phillips or flathead, depending on your drain type)
- A zip-it tool (a cheap, barbed plastic strip made for exactly this job) or needle-nose pliers
- Rubber gloves
- A bucket or trash bag for the gunk you pull out
- A kettle or pot of boiling water

## Step 1: Remove the drain stopper or cover

Bathtub drains come in a few styles: a simple push-pull stopper, a lift-and-turn stopper, or a trip-lever style connected to an overflow plate. For push-pull or lift-and-turn stoppers, they usually unscrew or lift out by hand. For a trip-lever style, you'll need to remove the overflow plate screws on the wall of the tub and pull out the entire linkage assembly, since that's often where hair actually accumulates rather than at the visible drain opening.

## Step 2: Pull out the visible clog

With gloves on, reach into the drain opening as far as you comfortably can and pull out any hair or debris you can see and reach. This alone often removes a surprising amount of the blockage.

## Step 3: Use a zip-it tool for what you can't reach

Slide a zip-it tool down into the drain past where you can reach by hand. The barbs along its length are designed to grab hair and gunk as you pull it back out. Work it in a few times, twisting slightly as you go, and expect it to come out looking pretty unpleasant. That's the point. Needle-nose pliers work as a substitute if you don't have a zip-it tool on hand, though they don't reach quite as far.

## Step 4: Flush with boiling water, then reassemble

Once you've pulled out as much debris as you can, pour a kettle or pot of boiling water slowly down the drain. This helps clear any remaining soap scum or grease residue clinging to the pipe walls that contributed to the clog in the first place. Pour it in stages rather than all at once, giving it time to work through.

Put the stopper or overflow plate back in place, run water for a minute to confirm it's draining at a normal speed, and you're done.

## Why this beats chemical drain cleaners for a tub

Chemical drain cleaners work through a caustic reaction, and while they can sometimes break down a clog, they come with real downsides:

- They can be harsh on older metal pipes, especially in homes with original galvanized or cast iron plumbing
- They don't do much for a clog that's mostly hair and soap scum wrapped into a solid mass, since there's often not much to chemically dissolve
- Repeated use can degrade rubber seals and gaskets over time
- They pose a real safety risk if they don't fully clear and you need to try a mechanical method afterward, since standing chemical residue can splash back

The zip-it and boiling water method addresses the actual physical clog directly, without introducing anything caustic into your plumbing.

## Preventing the next clog

A simple mesh drain cover placed over the tub drain catches hair before it goes down at all, which is by far the easiest way to avoid repeating this whole process every few months.
