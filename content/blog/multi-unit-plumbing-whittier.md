## The unique challenges of multi-unit plumbing

**Shared infrastructure.** A multi-unit building's main sewer line, water main, and central water heater serve every tenant. When the main goes down, every unit is affected. These aren't the kind of problems you can solve with a weekend DIY session — they require a commercial plumber with equipment like sewer cameras, hydro jetters, and the licensing to work on shared building systems.

**Preventive maintenance at scale.** The most cost-effective approach to multi-unit plumbing is prevention. Scheduled water heater inspections, drain cleaning programs, fixture audits, and sewer line camera inspections catch small issues before they become expensive emergencies. For a 50-unit building, preventive maintenance costs a fraction of what a single emergency call-out would cost.

**After-hours emergencies.** Tenants don't experience plumbing problems on a 9-to-5 schedule. A Saturday night sewer backup or a Sunday morning water heater failure needs a plumber who answers the phone and shows up — not one that sends you to voicemail until Monday.

**Tenant turnover and unit prep.** Between tenants, units need fixture inspections, drain cleaning, and sometimes fixture replacement. Having a plumber who can handle high-volume turnover prep efficiently keeps your units online and generating rent.

## Building a maintenance plan that works

Start with a comprehensive inspection of your building's plumbing systems. Document what you find — the age and condition of water heaters, the type and condition of sewer lines, any recurring issues in specific units. From there, build a schedule: quarterly drain cleaning, annual water heater inspections, bi-annual sewer camera inspections. Track everything, because a well-documented maintenance history is one of the best selling points when it's time to market the property.
