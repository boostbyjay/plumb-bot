Manhattan Beach is one of the South Bay's most desirable communities — beachfront properties, the Strand, the downtown corridor, and family neighborhoods filled with well-maintained homes. But living steps from the ocean comes with plumbing challenges that inland homeowners never deal with.

## Coastal plumbing challenges in Manhattan Beach

**Salt air corrosion.** This is the biggest issue for Manhattan Beach properties. Salt-laden ocean air accelerates corrosion on exterior plumbing fixtures, outdoor faucets, copper pipes in exterior walls, and pool equipment. Homes within a few blocks of the beach need more frequent inspection and replacement of exposed plumbing components. Stainless steel and PVC are preferred materials for outdoor applications in this environment.

**Aging infrastructure near downtown.** The area around Manhattan Beach Boulevard, the downtown corridor, and older residential streets has homes built in the 1950s through 1970s. Original galvanized piping, aging water heaters, and clay sewer lines are common findings.

**High water table.** Proximity to the ocean means a higher water table, which can affect basement and crawlspace plumbing, sump pump needs, and slab foundation conditions.

**Vacation rental stress.** Manhattan Beach has a significant number of vacation rentals and second homes. High turnover means plumbing systems see heavier use than primary residences, and deferred maintenance is common between property managers and owners.

## What Manhattan Beach homeowners should know

- Schedule annual inspections of exterior plumbing if you're within 5 blocks of the beach
- Consider replacing galvanized pipes before they fail
- Flush your water heater annually
- If you have a pool, maintain the plumbing system year-round — saltwater pools need specialized plumbing attention
- After any significant rainstorm, check for slow drains that could indicate sewer line issues
