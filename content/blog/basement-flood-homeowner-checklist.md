If you're reading this because water is actively rising in your basement right now, skip to the safety steps first. Everything else can wait a few minutes; your safety can't.

## Step 1: Electrical Safety Comes First

Water and electricity are a dangerous combination, and this is not the moment to rush toward the water to save belongings. Before anything else:

- **Do not enter standing water** if you're unsure whether it's in contact with electrical outlets, cords, or a plugged-in appliance
- **If you can safely reach your breaker panel without walking through water**, shut off power to the affected area
- **If the breaker panel is in the flooded area itself**, do not wade in to reach it. Call your utility company or an electrician instead, and stay out of the water until power is confirmed off

This step isn't optional and it isn't overly cautious. Electrical shock in standing water is a genuine, serious risk.

## Step 2: Identify and Stop the Water Source, If You Can

Once it's safe, try to figure out where the water is coming from:

- **A failed sump pump**: check if the pump is running at all, and whether the discharge line is blocked
- **A burst or leaking pipe**: if so, shut off your home's main water valve immediately
- **Groundwater intrusion from heavy rain**: harder to stop directly, but confirms the sump system needs attention

If the source is a plumbing failure rather than groundwater, shutting off the water supply stops the situation from getting worse while you deal with what's already happened.

## Step 3: Start Removing Water

Once the source is addressed or contained, begin removing standing water:

- A wet/dry shop vacuum works for smaller amounts
- A portable sump or utility pump handles larger volumes faster
- Open windows and use fans to start air circulation, which helps prevent mold growth

Speed matters here. Mold can begin developing within 24 to 48 hours in wet conditions, so the faster water is out and the area is drying, the better.

## Step 4: Document Everything for Insurance

Before you start throwing out ruined items or moving things around too much, take photos and video of the damage as it stands. This includes:

- Standing water levels and affected areas
- Damaged belongings, furniture, and flooring
- Any visible source of the water, like a burst pipe or malfunctioning pump

Insurance claims move faster and more smoothly with clear documentation from the start, and it's much harder to reconstruct after the cleanup is already done.

## Step 5: Know When Standing Water Needs Professional Extraction

For a small amount of water, a wet/dry vacuum and some fans might genuinely be enough. But if you're dealing with several inches of standing water, water that's been sitting for more than a day, or water that may have contacted sewage or contaminated groundwater, this moves beyond a DIY cleanup. Professional water extraction equipment removes water faster and more thoroughly than consumer tools, which matters directly for how much mold and structural damage you end up with.

## After the Water Is Gone

Once things are dry, take a hard look at why it happened. A one-time freak storm is different from a sump pump that clearly failed or a system that was never adequate for your home's groundwater situation.
