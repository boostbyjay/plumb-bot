Nothing happens when you flip the disposal switch, not even a hum. Before you start worrying about a dead motor or an electrical problem, there's a fix so simple it feels almost too easy: the built-in reset button. It genuinely takes less than a minute, and it solves this exact problem constantly.

## Why Disposals Have a Reset Button at All

Garbage disposals have a small thermal overload switch built into the motor housing. Its job is to protect the motor from burning out if it overheats, which can happen after grinding something tough, running for too long, or straining against a partial jam. When the motor gets too hot, this switch automatically trips and cuts power, even if the wall switch is still on. The reset button restores that connection once the motor has cooled.

## Finding the Button

Look underneath your disposal, at the bottom of the unit closest to the floor of the cabinet. You're looking for a small button, usually red or black, roughly the size of a pencil eraser. It's easy to miss with just a glance, so a flashlight helps.

## How to Reset It

1. **Turn off the wall switch** for the disposal before reaching underneath.
2. **Locate the reset button** on the bottom of the unit.
3. **Press it firmly** until you feel or hear a slight click.
4. **Wait a minute or two** if the unit feels warm to the touch, giving the motor a moment to fully cool.
5. **Turn the wall switch back on** and test with water running.

That's it. If the disposal was simply overheated and has now cooled, it should spin up normally.

## If the Reset Button Doesn't Fix It

If pressing reset doesn't bring the disposal back to life, or if it runs briefly and then trips again right away, there's likely a jam or obstruction causing the motor to overwork itself, which is why it keeps overheating in the first place. In that case, you'll want to manually free the flywheel with a hex key before resetting again, since resetting alone won't fix a physical blockage.

## What Repeated Tripping Is Telling You

A reset button that needs pressing once in a blue moon, maybe after grinding something a little tougher than usual, is completely normal. But if you find yourself resetting the disposal every week, or every time you use it, that's not a quirk to just live with. It usually points to one of a few underlying issues:

- A recurring jam that keeps straining the motor
- An electrical problem drawing too much current
- A motor that's aging out and running hotter than it should

## When to Stop Resetting and Get It Checked

Repeated tripping is your disposal telling you something is wrong beyond a one-time overheat. Continuing to reset it over and over without addressing the cause can shorten the unit's remaining life or, in some cases, point to a wiring issue worth taking seriously.
