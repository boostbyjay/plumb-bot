## What California health code says about grease traps

California's health code (CalCode) requires all food service establishments to have a properly sized and maintained grease trap or grease interceptor. The code specifies:

- **Grease traps** (for smaller kitchens) need pumping when the floating fat, oil, and grease layer plus settled solids occupy 25% or more of the trap's capacity
- **Grease interceptors** (for larger operations) follow the same 25% threshold but on a larger scale
- **Record keeping** is mandatory — health inspectors will ask for your maintenance logs
- **Failure to maintain** can result in health code violations, fines, or temporary closure

## How often does a grease trap need pumping?

There's no one-size-fits-all answer. It depends on your kitchen's volume, the types of food you prepare, and the size of your trap. A high-volume fried chicken restaurant needs more frequent service than a small sandwich shop. Most Anaheim restaurants find that quarterly or bi-monthly pumping keeps them safely under the 25% threshold, but some high-volume kitchens need monthly service.

## Signs your grease trap is overdue

- Slow-draining floor drains in the kitchen and prep area
- Foul, greasy odors coming from floor drains
- Water backing up into sinks or floor drains during peak prep times
- Visible grease buildup around drain covers
- Health inspection warnings about FOG (fats, oils, and grease) buildup
