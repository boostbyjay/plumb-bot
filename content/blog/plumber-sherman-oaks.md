Sherman Oaks has earned its reputation as one of the San Fernando Valley's best addresses. Established neighborhoods, the Ventura Boulevard corridor, and a mix of mid-century and newer homes make it a community where plumbing maintenance is part of responsible homeownership. But Sherman Oaks' homes, many built in the 1950s through 1970s, are starting to show their age.

## Sherman Oaks plumbing challenges

**Mid-century plumbing systems.** Most Sherman Oaks homes were built during the Valley's post-war boom. Original galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain stacks, and clay sewer lines are common findings. Galvanized pipes have been in service for 60+ years in many cases, and inside corrosion has likely narrowed the pipe diameter significantly.

**Valley hard water.** The San Fernando Valley's water supply is hard. Water heaters lose efficiency to sediment buildup, aerators get clogged with scale, and appliance lifespan is reduced. Annual water heater flushing is non-negotiable in Sherman Oaks homes.

**Sewer line age.** Clay sewer mains running from the street to the house have been in the ground since the 1950s or 60s. Tree roots from mature Sherman Oaks landscaping infiltrate joints, causing blockages. A camera inspection every few years catches problems before they become emergencies.

**HVAC and water heater space.** Many Sherman Oaks homes have water heaters and HVAC equipment in tight garage or attic spaces. These confined installations make maintenance and replacement more challenging and should be handled by experienced professionals.

## Tips for Sherman Oaks homeowners

- Flush your water heater annually to fight hard water sediment
- Schedule sewer camera inspections every 2-3 years for homes over 40 years old
- Don't ignore slow drains — they're often an early warning sign of sewer line problems
- Track your water bill for unexplained increases
