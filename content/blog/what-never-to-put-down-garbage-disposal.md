A garbage disposal feels like it can handle just about anything you scrape off a plate, which is exactly why so many of them jam, dull, or burn out well before their time. The truth is that a small list of common foods causes the vast majority of disposal problems. Knowing what to keep out of it is the single best thing you can do to make it last.

## 1. Grease, Fat, and Cooking Oil

This is the number one offender, and not because it damages the disposal directly. Grease goes down as a liquid but cools and hardens as it travels through your pipes, coating the inside of the drain line and the disposal chamber. Over time it narrows the pipe and traps other debris against it, creating a stubborn clog that has nothing to do with the disposal's grinding ability. Pour grease into a can or jar and toss it in the trash instead.

## 2. Fibrous Vegetables

Celery, corn husks, asparagus ends, and onion skins are full of long, stringy fibers that tend to wrap around the spinning components instead of getting chopped up. Those fibers can tangle the blades, strain the motor, and jam the flywheel entirely.

## 3. Coffee Grounds

Coffee grounds seem harmless because they're so fine, but in volume they behave almost like sand or wet cement. They don't break down further in the disposal, and they can accumulate in the trap and pipes below, forming a dense sludge that's tough to clear.

## 4. Eggshells

This one's debated, and some disposal manufacturers say the membrane inside eggshells can actually stick to disposal components rather than getting fully pulverized. Whether or not eggshells damage the blades, the ground-up shell fragments contribute to buildup in your pipes over time. It's simple enough to compost them or toss them instead.

## 5. Pasta and Rice

These expand when they absorb water, which is exactly what happens if they sit in your drain line after going down the disposal. A modest amount of leftover pasta can swell into a soft, dense clog that packs the pipe from the inside out.

## 6. Bones

Small, hard bone fragments are one of the fastest ways to jam or chip a disposal's blades. Disposals are designed to macerate soft food waste, not to function like a bone grinder, and repeated attempts to force bones through will shorten the unit's life significantly.

## A Quick Reference

- Grease, fat, oil: hardens in pipes and causes clogs downstream
- Celery, corn husks, onion skins: fibers tangle the blades and flywheel
- Coffee grounds: builds sludge in the trap and drain line
- Eggshells: contributes to buildup, may stick to internal parts
- Pasta, rice: expands and clogs pipes after grinding
- Bones: jams and chips blades, strains the motor

## Running It Right

Even with safe foods, always run cold water before, during, and after grinding. Cold water keeps any grease solid enough to get chopped up and flushed through rather than melting and coating the pipe walls. Grind in small batches rather than dumping in a full plate's worth of scraps at once.

Follow this list and your disposal will likely outlast its expected lifespan with no drama at all.
