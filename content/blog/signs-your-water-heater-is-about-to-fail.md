A water heater failure at 6 a.m. before work is one of the least fun ways to start a day. The good news is that most tanks give plenty of warning before they actually quit. Here's what to watch for so a slow decline doesn't turn into a sudden flood in your utility closet.

## Signs coming from the water itself

1. **Rusty or discolored water.** If hot water comes out with a brownish or reddish tint while cold water stays clear, that's often rust forming inside the tank itself, not just in old pipes. It usually means the tank's interior lining has started to break down, and once that happens, corrosion tends to accelerate rather than slow down.
2. **A rotten egg smell from the hot water.** This usually points to bacteria reacting with the anode rod inside the tank, particularly with well water. It's not always a sign of imminent failure, but it does mean the tank's internal environment has changed in a way worth having checked.
3. **Inconsistent water temperature.** Water that runs hot, then lukewarm, then hot again during a single shower often points to a failing heating element (electric units) or a worn-out thermostat or burner assembly (gas units).

## Signs coming from the tank itself

4. **Rumbling or popping sounds.** Sediment settles at the bottom of a tank over years of use, and as it hardens, it creates pockets that trap water. When the burner or element heats that trapped water, it creates the popping or rumbling sound many homeowners describe as the tank "boiling." It's a sign of accumulated wear that makes the tank work harder and fail sooner.
5. **Water pooling near the base.** Any puddle at the base of your water heater deserves immediate attention. It could be a loose fitting, a failing drain valve, or worse, a hairline crack in the tank itself, which cannot be repaired.
6. **Visible rust on the tank exterior.** Surface rust on the tank shell, especially near fittings or seams, often mirrors what's happening inside, and is a strong visual cue that the tank's structural integrity is declining.

## Signs tied to age and cost

7. **Age over 10 to 12 years.** Most standard tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years. If yours is in or past that range, age alone puts it at meaningfully higher risk of failure, even if it seems to be running fine right now. Check the serial number on the manufacturer's label; it usually encodes the manufacture date.
8. **Rising energy bills without a clear reason.** As sediment builds up and components wear down, your water heater has to work harder to produce the same amount of hot water. If your gas or electric bill has crept up and you haven't changed your usage habits, an aging water heater is a common, overlooked cause.

## What to do with this list

None of these signs alone means your water heater will fail tomorrow, but two or three together are a strong signal to start planning a replacement on your own timeline instead of waiting for an emergency.
