Most clogs don't happen overnight. They build gradually, one layer of grease, hair, or soap scum at a time, until one day a drain that used to work fine suddenly doesn't. The tricky part is that by the time a clog is obvious, it's usually further along than most homeowners assume. Here are seven signs worth paying attention to, and what each one tells you about how serious the buildup actually is.

## 1. Water drains slower than it used to

This is the earliest and most common sign, and it's easy to write off as normal. If a sink or tub that used to drain in seconds now takes several seconds longer, that's not your imagination. It means something inside the pipe, usually hair, grease, or soap residue, is narrowing the space water has to pass through.

## 2. Gurgling sounds after you drain a fixture

A gurgle is trapped air trying to escape past a partial blockage as water forces its way past. It's a step up from simple slowness because it means the clog has narrowed the pipe enough to disrupt normal airflow, not just water flow.

## 3. A persistent bad odor near the drain

Standing water and trapped organic material, food scraps, hair, soap scum, start to break down and produce a distinct sour or musty smell. If you notice an odor specifically near a drain (rather than the whole room), it's a strong sign there's buildup sitting inside the pipe rather than a fully clear path.

## 4. Water backing up in a different fixture

This one tends to get people's attention fast. If running the washing machine causes water to bubble up in a nearby floor drain, or flushing the toilet causes the tub to gurgle or fill slightly, that's a sign the blockage is downstream of where multiple drain lines connect, not just isolated to one fixture.

## 5. Small flying insects near the drain

Drain flies and fruit flies are drawn to the organic buildup, grease, food particles, hair, and soap scum, that clings to the inside of a slow-draining pipe. If you keep noticing tiny bugs near a sink or floor drain no matter how often you clean the visible surfaces, there's very likely material trapped further down that you can't see.

## 6. A clog that keeps coming back

If you've cleared a drain once, maybe with a plunger or a store-bought cleaner, and it clogs again within days or weeks, that's telling you the clearing method only removed part of the blockage, or it addressed a symptom rather than the underlying buildup further down the line.

## 7. Multiple drains acting up around the same time

One slow drain is usually isolated. Several slow drains across the house, especially ones on lower floors or connected to the same general plumbing run, point toward a blockage in a shared line rather than separate, unrelated clogs.

## What to do with this information

A single sign, especially just slower drainage, is often fine to address with basic tools like a plunger or a drain-cleaning tool. But as you check more boxes on this list, particularly gurgling, backups in other fixtures, or multiple drains at once, you're looking at a more significant blockage that basic DIY methods are less likely to fully clear.
