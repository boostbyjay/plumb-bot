Pasadena is one of the oldest incorporated cities in Los Angeles County, and its housing stock tells that story. Walk through any neighborhood near Old Town Pasadena or the bungalow courts off Mission Street, and you're looking at homes built between 1910 and 1940. Many of them still have some of the original plumbing — galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain stacks, and clay sewer lines that have been in the ground for nearly a century. That history is part of what makes Pasadena charming, but it also means plumbing problems here tend to be more complex than in newer cities.

## What makes Pasadena plumbing unique

**Older infrastructure.** Galvanized pipes were standard through the 1960s, and they don't last forever. Inside diameter narrows over decades of mineral deposits and corrosion, leading to low water pressure and eventual failure. If your Pasadena home still has sections of galvanized pipe, it's only a matter of time before replacement becomes necessary.

**Mature tree root systems.** Pasadena's famous oak and sycamore trees are beautiful, but their roots aggressively seek out water — and old clay sewer lines are an easy target. Root intrusion is one of the most common sewer line problems we see in Pasadena homes, causing slow drains, backups, and expensive repairs if left untreated.

**Hard water.** The San Gabriel Valley sits on hard water. Mineral buildup shortens water heater life, clogs aerators, and leaves calcium deposits on fixtures throughout your home. A water softener or regular descaling maintenance makes a real difference in Pasadena households.

## Warning signs you shouldn't ignore

- **Water pressure dropping suddenly** — could be a failing water heater, a developing leak, or galvanized pipes narrowing further
- **Gurgling drains** — often an early sign of sewer line trouble or vent stack blockage
- **Warm spots on the floor** — may indicate a hot water line leak under a slab foundation
- **Brown or rusty water** from faucets — points to corroding galvanized supply lines
- **Recurring clogs** in the same drain — suggests a deeper issue in the sewer line rather than a surface clog

## Tips for Pasadena homeowners

If you own a home built before 1960 in Pasadena, schedule a camera inspection of your sewer line every few years. Catching a crack or root intrusion early means trenchless repair instead of a full replacement — a difference of thousands of dollars. Keep an eye on your water bill month to month; a gradual increase with no change in usage is often the first sign of a hidden leak.
