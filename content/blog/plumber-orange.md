Orange calls itself the "City of Orange" — and for good reason. It's one of the oldest cities in Orange County, with a charming Old Town district, historic neighborhoods filled with Craftsman and Spanish Revival homes, and a growing commercial corridor along Katella Avenue and Chapman Avenue. All that history and growth means Orange's plumbing runs the full spectrum, from century-old pipes in the historic district to modern systems in newer developments.

## Orange's plumbing landscape

**Historic homes in Old Town and surrounding neighborhoods.** Many homes in Orange's historic districts were built between the 1880s and 1940s. Original galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain stacks, and clay sewer lines are common. These homes need a plumber who respects the property's character while addressing functional issues — someone who knows how to modernize a plumbing system without destroying original fixtures and finishes.

**Orange County hard water.** Hard water is a consistent challenge across Orange County. Mineral buildup in water heaters, aerators, and pipes reduces efficiency and shortens the lifespan of plumbing fixtures. Many Orange homeowners benefit from water softener installation or regular descaling maintenance.

**Growing commercial district.** Chapman Avenue, Katella Avenue, and the area around The Outlets at Orange have seen significant commercial development. Restaurants, retail spaces, and office buildings all need commercial plumbing services — from fixture installation to grease trap maintenance to preventive maintenance contracts.

## Common Orange plumbing issues

- **Water heater failures** from sediment buildup in hard water
- **Sewer line problems** in older Clay pipes near historic districts
- **Low water pressure** from corroding galvanized pipes
- **Drain clogs** in older homes with narrower pipes
- **Slab leaks** in homes on concrete foundations

## Tips for Orange homeowners

Schedule a sewer camera inspection every few years if your home is over 50 years old. Flush your water heater annually. Keep an eye on your water bill — a sudden increase often signals a hidden leak.
