There's a short window every fall when the weather is still mild enough to comfortably do outdoor maintenance, but cold enough that winter is clearly on its way. That window is exactly when you want to handle a handful of plumbing tasks that get a lot harder, or a lot more urgent, once temperatures drop for good.

## Clean your gutters

This one doesn't look like a plumbing task, but it directly affects your home's relationship with water. Clogged gutters overflow near your foundation instead of directing water away from the house through downspouts, and that water has a way of finding its way into basements and crawlspaces over time. Clear out leaves and debris now, before the first freeze makes the job miserable and before heavy fall rains have a chance to cause damage.

## Disconnect and store garden hoses

A hose left connected to an outdoor faucet traps water that has nowhere to expand once it freezes, and that's a common cause of cracked spigots. Disconnect every hose, drain it fully, and coil it up somewhere protected for the winter. While you're out there, go ahead and shut off any interior valves feeding outdoor faucets if your home has them.

## Inspect your water heater before heavy winter use

Winter tends to mean more hot water use, longer showers, more laundry, more dishes from holiday cooking. Before that demand ramps up, give your water heater a once-over:

- Check for rust or moisture around the base of the tank
- Listen for popping or rumbling sounds that suggest sediment buildup
- Confirm the temperature setting is where you expect it (120°F is a common, safe default)
- Note the age of the unit if you can find it, most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years

Catching a struggling water heater in the fall means you're not stuck without hot water during the coldest week of the year.

## Insulate exposed pipes

If you haven't already, this is the time to add foam pipe insulation to any exposed pipes in crawlspaces, attics, garages, or along exterior walls. It's a simple task that takes far less effort than dealing with a frozen or burst pipe later.

## Test your sump pump

If your area sees a mix of fall rain and early freezes, make sure your sump pump is ready. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the float switch triggers the pump properly and water clears through the discharge line.

## Schedule a pre-winter inspection

A lot of small plumbing issues, a slightly loose fitting, a valve that's starting to corrode, minor sediment buildup in the water heater, are much easier to spot and fix in the fall than in the middle of winter when a burst pipe or a failed water heater becomes an emergency instead of a routine repair. A professional inspection before the season fully turns can catch these before they become urgent.

## Your fall checklist at a glance

1. Clean gutters and check downspout drainage
2. Disconnect, drain, and store garden hoses
3. Inspect the water heater for age, sound, and leaks
4. Insulate exposed pipes in unheated areas
5. Test the sump pump
6. Book a pre-winter plumbing inspection

Fall maintenance is really about trading a little bit of easy work now for a much smoother winter later.
