Toilets are one of those fixtures homeowners rarely think about until something goes wrong, and by then it's often an unpleasant surprise. The truth is that most toilet failures don't happen suddenly out of nowhere. There are usually warning signs for weeks or months beforehand, quiet clues that get dismissed as minor quirks until the underlying issue finally catches up. Here are five worth paying attention to.

## 1. A base that rocks or wobbles

Press down gently on opposite sides of the toilet base. Any noticeable rocking, even slight, means it isn't sitting flush and stable on the floor. Sometimes this is as simple as loose closet bolts (the bolts anchoring the toilet to the floor flange), an easy fix. But persistent rocking can also mean the wax ring seal underneath has failed or the subfloor itself has weakened from water damage, both of which are more involved to address and only get worse the longer they're ignored.

## 2. Hairline cracks in the porcelain

Porcelain doesn't fail gradually the way some materials do. A small hairline crack, especially in the tank or at the base, can look cosmetic and unimportant for a long time and then suddenly propagate into a full break, often triggered by nothing more dramatic than normal daily use or a minor temperature change. Any visible crack, no matter how small, is worth taking seriously rather than watching to see if it gets worse.

## 3. Water pooling at the base

If you notice water collecting around the base of the toilet, especially right after a flush, that's a strong sign the wax ring seal between the toilet and the floor flange has failed. This seal is what keeps wastewater contained as it exits the toilet into the drain pipe below, and a compromised seal means water (and eventually, potential structural damage to your subfloor) is going somewhere it shouldn't.

## 4. Running that keeps coming back despite repairs

If you've replaced the flapper, adjusted the fill valve, and the toilet still runs, or the running problem keeps returning within weeks of each repair, that pattern suggests something more fundamental is wearing out, such as the flush valve seat itself, rather than any individual replaceable part. Repeated repairs that don't hold are the toilet's way of telling you it's nearing the end of its functional life, or that a specific internal component needs professional attention.

## 5. Age over 25 years

Toilets are durable, but they're not permanent. Older models, especially those installed before more modern water-efficient designs became standard, often use more water per flush and are more prone to internal wear on parts that may not even be manufactured anymore, making repairs harder to source. If your toilet has been in place for a quarter century or more, it's worth budgeting mentally for the possibility that a repair might not be the most practical fix, especially if you're also seeing any of the signs above.

## Why cracks deserve extra caution

Of everything on this list, a cracked toilet is the one that can turn from a minor issue to a real problem the fastest. Unlike a slow leak you can monitor over days, a stressed hairline crack in porcelain can give way suddenly, sometimes releasing a full tank's worth of water onto your bathroom floor with no advance warning. If you spot a crack, don't wait to see how it develops.

## Putting it together

One sign on its own, especially minor rocking from loose bolts, is often an easy fix. Multiple signs together, particularly a wobbly base combined with water pooling at the floor, point toward a wax ring or flange issue that needs more than a quick DIY tightening.
