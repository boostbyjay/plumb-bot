Culver City has transformed from a modest industrial suburb into one of the Westside's most desirable addresses. The media industry boom, the downtown revitalization, and the city's unbeatable location between LAX and the rest of the Westside have brought new construction, renovated historic homes, and a growing commercial sector — all of which need plumbing services.

## Culver City's dual personality

**Historic homes.** Culver City has a growing inventory of historic homes, particularly in the downtown area and surrounding residential streets. Many were built in the 1920s through 1940s and feature original plumbing — galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains, and clay sewer lines. These homes need a plumber who can upgrade systems while preserving the character of the property.

**New construction and development.** The media industry expansion has driven significant new construction in Culver City. Modern apartments, condos, and mixed-use buildings have different plumbing needs — commercial-grade systems, high-volume fixtures, and building-wide infrastructure that requires a commercial plumber's expertise.

**Commercial plumbing demand.** Culver City's restaurant scene, office buildings, and production studio facilities all need commercial plumbing services. Grease traps, backflow testing, preventive maintenance, and emergency response are in constant demand from Culver City businesses.

## What Culver City homeowners should know

- Older Culver City homes should have sewer camera inspections every few years
- Water heater maintenance is essential — hard water affects Westside homes too
- If you're renovating a historic Culver City home, work with a plumber who understands older construction methods
