Glendale is one of the more diverse housing markets in Los Angeles County. Drive through the Verdugo Woodlands and you'll find Craftsman homes from the 1920s with original plumbing. Head toward the hills and you'll see mid-century ranches and custom builds from the 1960s and 70s. Downtown Glendale and the area around the Galleria have newer apartment complexes and mixed-use buildings. Each of these housing types brings its own set of plumbing considerations, and Glendale's location in the foothills adds some challenges you won't find in flatland communities.

## Hard water and water pressure

Glendale's water comes from the LA Department of Water and Power and the Metropolitan Water District — both known for hard water. Mineral buildup in pipes and water heaters is a consistent issue across Glendale neighborhoods. Homes in the hillside areas also frequently deal with water pressure problems, especially during peak usage hours, because gravity-fed systems have to work harder to push water uphill.

## Sewer line challenges

Older Glendale homes often have clay or cast iron sewer lines that have been in the ground for 50 to 80 years. Tree roots from the area's mature jacaranda, oak, and pine trees regularly infiltrate these old lines, causing blockages and structural damage. If you're noticing recurring drain clogs, gurgling sounds, or sewage odors, a sewer camera inspection is the fastest way to identify the problem.

## Hillside plumbing considerations

Glendale's hillside homes face some unique issues. Soil movement on slopes can stress underground pipes, leading to leaks and breaks that are harder to access than in flat-yard properties. Foundation settlement can also shift pipes and fittings, creating leaks that show up as wet spots in the yard or unexplained water bill increases. If you live in the Glendale hills, pay extra attention to sudden changes in water pressure or new wet spots on your property.

## Preventive maintenance for Glendale homes

- Flush your water heater annually to combat sediment from hard water
- Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2-3 years for homes over 40 years old
- Keep gutters and downspouts clear — Glendale's hillside runoff can stress foundation drainage
- Test your sump pump before the rainy season, especially if your home is on a slope
