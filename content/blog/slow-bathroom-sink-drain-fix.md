If your bathroom sink has gone from draining instantly to leaving a little pool of water behind after every use, you almost certainly don't need a plumber yet. Bathroom sinks clog for a very predictable reason: hair, combined with toothpaste residue, soap scum, and skincare product buildup, gets trapped right at the pop-up stopper and slowly builds into a dense mat that blocks water flow. The fix is usually a hands-on job you can knock out in about fifteen minutes.

## Why bathroom sinks clog differently than kitchen sinks

Kitchen drains usually deal with grease and food particles further down the line. Bathroom sink clogs, by contrast, tend to form right at the stopper mechanism itself, since that's exactly where hair gets caught as water swirls down. That's good news, because it means the fix is often as simple as pulling the stopper out and cleaning it, rather than dealing with something buried deep in the pipe.

## What you'll need

- A pair of pliers or an old toothbrush
- A "zip-it" style drain-cleaning tool (a flexible plastic strip with barbs, sold cheaply at any hardware store) or a straightened wire hanger
- Rubber gloves
- Paper towels or an old rag
- A flashlight

## Step 1: Remove the pop-up stopper

Most pop-up stoppers lift straight out if you pull up and slightly twist. If yours doesn't budge, look underneath the sink for a horizontal rod (the pivot rod) connected to a nut behind the drain pipe. Unscrew that nut, pull the rod out slightly, and the stopper should lift free.

## Step 2: Clean off what you find

This is the unglamorous part. You'll typically find a tangled clump of hair wrapped around the base of the stopper, along with a slimy coating of soap scum and product residue. Pull it off by hand (gloves recommended) or with pliers, and wipe the stopper clean before setting it aside.

## Step 3: Send the zip-it tool down the drain

With the stopper out, feed the zip-it tool straight down into the drain opening. The small barbs along its length are designed to grab hair and debris as you slowly pull it back out. Do this a few times, rotating the tool slightly and pulling gently, until you're not bringing up any more material.

## Step 4: Flush with hot water

Once you've cleared out the visible buildup, run hot tap water for a minute or two to help flush any remaining loose debris further down the drain line.

## Step 5: Reinstall the stopper and test

Put the clean stopper back in the same way you removed it, reconnect the pivot rod if you had to disconnect it, and run water to confirm it drains at full speed with no pooling.

## Preventing this from happening again

The single best prevention step is a mesh drain catcher or hair strainer placed over or in the drain opening. It's an inexpensive product that catches hair before it ever reaches the stopper, and it means the next time you check, there's a lot less to clean off.

## When it's not just the stopper

If you've cleared the stopper and cleaned out what the zip-it tool could reach, but the sink is still draining slowly, the clog may extend further down into the P-trap or the branch line beyond it. That's a bit more involved to access and clear properly.
