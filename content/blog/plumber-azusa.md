Azusa sits at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains, and its plumbing reflects that unique position. The city has a mix of older residential neighborhoods, foothill homes with special considerations, and a growing commercial district along Azusa Avenue. For Azusa homeowners, a few specific plumbing issues tend to come up more often than in other parts of the valley.

## What sets Azusa plumbing apart

**Foothill water pressure.** Azusa's elevation and proximity to the mountains mean some neighborhoods experience different water pressure characteristics than the flat valley floor. Homes in the hillside areas may have lower incoming pressure, which affects shower performance, appliance operation, and the demands placed on water heaters and pressure-balancing valves.

**Older sewer infrastructure.** Like many SGV cities, Azusa has numerous homes with clay sewer lines. The combination of older piping, mature trees, and ground movement in the foothill areas creates conditions where sewer line problems are more likely.

**Hard water.** Azusa shares the San Gabriel Valley's hard water supply. Water heater efficiency, fixture lifespan, and pipe interior condition are all affected by mineral buildup over time.

**Seismic considerations.** Being closer to the San Andreas Fault zone means Azusa homes may experience more ground movement than other areas. Pipe joints and fittings can shift over time, leading to leaks that are harder to detect and reach.

## Tips for Azusa homeowners

- Flush your water heater annually to combat sediment from hard water
- Schedule sewer camera inspections for older homes
- Know where your main shutoff valve is
- After any significant earthquake, check your home for water leaks, gas smells, and water heater damage
