Whittier is one of the older cities in Los Angeles County, and its residential neighborhoods are packed with homes that have real character — and real plumbing challenges. From the Craftsman bungalows in Uptown Whittier to the post-war ranches and mid-century homes spreading toward Pico Rivera and Santa Fe Springs, Whittier's housing stock includes plenty of properties where the original plumbing has been patched, repaired, and patched again over the decades.

## What you're likely dealing with in a Whittier home

**Galvanized and cast iron pipes.** Many Whittier homes built before 1970 still have sections of galvanized supply piping and cast iron drain stacks. Galvanized pipes corrode from the inside, gradually narrowing the opening and reducing water pressure. Cast iron drain lines can develop cracks and root intrusion over decades of service.

**Original water heaters.** If your water heater is the same age as your house, it's overdue for replacement. Most water heaters last 8-12 years, and pushing past that limit means risking a tank failure that floods your home.

**Aging sewer lines.** Clay sewer mains running from the street to your house are common in Whittier's older neighborhoods. Roots from mature landscaping infiltrate the joints, causing blockages that range from annoying (slow drains) to catastrophic (sewage backing up into the house).

**Slab foundation leaks.** Whittier has its share of concrete slab homes. When a hot or cold water line develops a pinhole leak under the slab, symptoms include a warm spot on the floor, a water bill that won't stop climbing, or the sound of running water with everything turned off.

## Tips for Whittier homeowners

Schedule a camera inspection of your sewer line every 2-3 years if your home is over 40 years old. Keep your water heater flushed annually — sediment buildup from the area's hard water will shorten its lifespan. And know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it in an emergency.
