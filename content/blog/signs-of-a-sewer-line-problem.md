Sewer line problems have a habit of showing up as small, easy-to-explain-away annoyances before they turn into a full backup. A slow drain here, an odd smell there. Individually, these things seem minor. Together, they can point to something happening in the main line that carries wastewater from your house to the municipal sewer or septic tank.

Here are the signs that deserve more attention than "I'll deal with it eventually."

## 1. Multiple drains clogging at the same time

If your kitchen sink, bathroom sink, and tub all start draining slowly within the same week, that's not a coincidence of bad luck. Individual fixture clogs happen in the pipe closest to that fixture. When several drains act up at once, especially ones on different floors or in different parts of the house, it usually points to a blockage further down the line, in the main sewer pipe that all of them share.

## 2. A persistent sewage odor

A whiff of sewer smell after a heavy rain isn't unusual, but a lingering rotten-egg or sewage smell inside or outside your home is worth investigating. It can mean a crack or break somewhere in the line is letting gas escape before it reaches the vent stack, or that wastewater isn't draining away properly and gases are backing up.

## 3. An unusually green, lush patch of lawn

This one surprises a lot of homeowners. If there's a patch of grass over or near your sewer line that's noticeably greener and more lush than the rest of your yard, especially during dry spells, it may be getting extra "fertilizer" from a leaking sewer pipe underground. It's a strange silver lining sign, but it's a real one.

## 4. Gurgling toilets or drains

When you run the washing machine or flush a toilet and hear gurgling from another fixture, like the bathtub or a floor drain, air is getting trapped somewhere in the system and forcing its way out through the path of least resistance. This is often related to a partial blockage in the main line disrupting normal airflow through the vent system.

## 5. Sewage backing up in the lowest drain

The lowest drain in your house, often a basement floor drain or a basement bathroom, is usually the first place a main line blockage shows up, because it's the low point where backed-up wastewater has nowhere else to go. A backup here is one of the more serious signs on this list and typically means the blockage is significant.

## What to do if you spot these signs

A few practical first steps if you're noticing any of the above:

- Stop running large amounts of water (laundry, dishwasher, long showers) until you know more
- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners, which won't reach a main line clog and can complicate a professional's diagnosis
- Note which fixtures are affected and in what order, since that detail helps narrow down where the problem is

Sewer line issues aren't something to troubleshoot with a plunger and hope. Diagnosing a main line problem typically requires a camera inspection to see exactly what's going on inside the pipe, whether that's tree roots, a collapsed section, or years of buildup.
