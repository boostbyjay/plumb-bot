When people think about checking for leaks, they usually picture crawling under the kitchen sink with a flashlight. Fair enough, that's a common spot. But some of the most damaging leaks happen in places nobody thinks to look, quietly soaking subfloors and drywall for weeks or months before anyone notices a stain or a smell. Here are six spots worth adding to your regular walk-through.

## 1. Behind the Washing Machine

The rubber or braided hoses feeding your washer sit under constant water pressure, even when the machine isn't running, and they're tucked behind a large appliance most people never pull out. A hose that's cracking or bulging can fail without warning. Pull the machine out once or twice a year and inspect both the hoses and the connections at the wall.

## 2. Under the Water Heater

Water heaters are usually shoved in a corner of a basement, garage, or closet and forgotten about until something's wrong. A slow leak from the tank itself, a loose drain valve, or a failing pressure relief valve can pool underneath for a long time, especially if there's no floor drain nearby. Check the area around the base every few months, and look for rust streaks or mineral crusting on fittings.

## 3. Irrigation Valve Boxes

If you have an in-ground sprinkler system, the valve boxes buried in your yard are easy to forget entirely. A cracked fitting inside one of these boxes can leak steadily underground, showing up only as a soggy patch of grass or a mysteriously high water bill. Pop the lid off each box once a season and look for standing water.

## 4. The Water Softener Bypass Valve

Water softeners have a bypass valve used during maintenance or when the system needs to be taken offline. These valves see infrequent use, which means seals can dry out or fittings can loosen without anyone noticing. Give it a visual check when you're refilling the salt tank.

## 5. The Ice Maker Line

That thin plastic or copper line running behind your refrigerator is easy to forget because it's hidden and rarely touched after installation. Kinks from moving the fridge, or age-related cracking in plastic lines, can cause a slow drip that seeps into flooring before it's visible. Pull the fridge out periodically to inspect the connection at both ends.

## 6. The Attic, If You Have a Whole-House Humidifier

Homes with a whole-house humidifier tied into the furnace often have a water line running up into the attic or mechanical space. Because nobody spends much time up there, a slow leak at a fitting can go undetected until it stains a ceiling below. A quick seasonal look with a flashlight is worth the climb.

## Why These Matter More Than the Obvious Spots

The leaks under your kitchen or bathroom sink usually announce themselves fast, a puddle you'll notice the next time you open the cabinet. The spots above are different because they're out of sight and out of your normal routine, which means small leaks have time to become big, expensive problems: warped subfloors, mold growth, and even structural damage in bad cases.

## Building a Simple Habit

You don't need a formal inspection schedule. Pick a day each season, maybe when you change your HVAC filter, and spend fifteen minutes walking through this list. It costs nothing and catches problems while they're still cheap to fix.
