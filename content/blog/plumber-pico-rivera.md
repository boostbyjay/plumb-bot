Pico Rivera is a well-established community in Los Angeles County — solid mid-century homes, family-oriented neighborhoods, and residents who expect honest service at fair prices. The plumbing in Pico Rivera homes tells the story of the city's development, primarily in the 1950s and 1960s, and those original systems need attention after decades of service.

## Common Pico Rivera plumbing issues

**Galvanized supply piping.** Many Pico Rivera homes still have sections of original galvanized pipe. Over 60-plus years, galvanized pipes corrode from the inside, gradually reducing water pressure and eventually requiring replacement. If your Pico Rivera home has low water pressure, galvanized pipes are the likely culprit.

**Cast iron and clay sewer lines.** The original sewer infrastructure in many Pico Rivera homes uses cast iron drain stacks and clay sewer mains. These materials last a long time but aren't invincible. Root intrusion from mature trees, internal corrosion, and ground movement can all cause failures.

**Water heater age.** With Pico Rivera homes averaging 60+ years old, the original water heater has almost certainly been replaced at least once. But if your current water heater is over 10 years old, it's on borrowed time — especially with the LA County area's hard water supply.

**Slab foundation leaks.** Many Pico Rivera homes sit on concrete slab foundations. Pinhole leaks in copper lines under the slab can cause significant damage over time. Watch for warm floor spots, unexplained water bill increases, or the sound of running water with everything turned off.

## Tips for Pico Rivera homeowners

- Flush your water heater annually
- Schedule a sewer camera inspection for homes over 40 years old
- Don't ignore recurring drain clogs — they're often a main line issue
- Keep your plumber's number handy
