Ask five people how often they clean their drains and you'll get five different answers, most of them somewhere between "never" and "only when it's already clogged." The honest answer depends on which drain you're talking about, since a kitchen sink, a bathroom sink, and a floor drain all collect different kinds of buildup at very different rates. Here's a realistic schedule, along with the simple at-home method for each.

## Kitchen sink: monthly

Kitchen drains deal with grease, oil, and small food particles more than any other drain in the house, and grease is uniquely good at coating pipe walls and hardening as it cools. Even with a garbage disposal doing its job, some residue makes it past and starts building up over time.

**A simple monthly method:** Run hot water for about 30 seconds, then pour a kettle of nearly boiling water directly down the drain to help soften and move along grease buildup before it hardens further. If you have a garbage disposal, running a handful of ice cubes through it periodically also helps knock loose residue off the blades and inner walls.

## Bathroom sink: every 2 to 3 months

Bathroom sinks accumulate hair, toothpaste residue, and soap scum, which builds more slowly than kitchen grease but eventually forms the same kind of dense clog, usually right at the pop-up stopper.

**A simple quarterly method:** Pull the pop-up stopper (see our guide on clearing a slow bathroom sink drain for the exact steps), clear off any hair wrapped around it, and run a zip-it tool a few inches into the drain opening to catch anything sitting just below.

## Shower and tub drains: every 2 to 3 months

Similar buildup pattern to bathroom sinks, hair being the primary culprit, but often at a larger volume given how much hair rinses off during a shower compared to hand washing.

**A simple quarterly method:** Remove the drain cover, pull any visible hair clumps, and use a hooked tool to clear a few inches further down. If you use a mesh hair catcher (highly recommended), just emptying it weekly can stretch out how often a full cleanout is needed.

## Floor drains (basement, laundry, garage): twice a year

Floor drains don't see daily use the way sink and shower drains do, but that's actually part of the problem. Infrequent use means the water in the trap can evaporate over time, which allows sewer gas odors to escape into the room, and dust or debris can settle and dry into the pipe undisturbed for months.

**A simple semi-annual method:** Pour a couple of gallons of water down the drain to refill the trap and check that it drains at a normal speed. If it's slow, a basic drain-cleaning tool can clear light buildup before it becomes a real clog.

## Outdoor drains: at the start of each season

If your home has exterior drains, like a driveway or patio drain, a quick seasonal check for leaves, dirt, and debris keeps them from becoming a bigger problem during heavy rain.

## Signs you need to clean sooner than scheduled

Regardless of the calendar, address a drain right away if you notice slower drainage than usual, a persistent odor, or gurgling. Those are your plumbing telling you buildup is ahead of schedule, and waiting for the "official" cleaning date isn't necessary once you see warning signs.

## When routine cleaning stops being enough

If you're following a reasonable schedule and a drain is still slowing down faster than expected, or a clog keeps returning despite regular maintenance, there may be a bigger blockage or a structural issue further down the line that routine cleaning won't reach.
