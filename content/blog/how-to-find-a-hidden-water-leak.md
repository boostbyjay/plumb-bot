Some of the most damaging leaks in a home are the ones you never see coming, because they're happening behind a wall, under a slab, or inside a cabinet you rarely open. By the time there's a visible sign, real damage has often already been done. Fortunately, your water meter can tell you whether a hidden leak exists before you ever spot a stain.

## The water meter test

This is the single most reliable DIY way to check for a leak anywhere in your home's plumbing.

1. Make sure no water is being used anywhere in the house. Turn off all faucets, don't run the dishwasher or washing machine, and confirm the ice maker isn't mid-cycle.
2. Find your water meter, usually located near the street in a small in-ground box, or sometimes in a basement or utility area.
3. Note the current reading, or check whether your meter has a small leak indicator, often a small triangle or wheel that spins whenever any water is flowing through the line.
4. Wait 30 minutes to an hour without using any water, then check again.

If the reading has changed, or the leak indicator has moved at all, water is flowing somewhere in your system even though nothing should be running. That confirms a leak exists; it just doesn't tell you where yet.

## Narrowing down the location

Once you know a leak exists, work through these common hiding spots:

- **Under sinks:** check supply lines, shutoff valves, and drain connections for dampness, staining, or a faint musty smell.
- **Behind appliances:** pull out the washing machine, refrigerator (if it has a water line), and dishwasher to check hoses and connections for wear or slow drips.
- **Around the water heater:** look for moisture at the base of the tank or along the fittings at the top.
- **Toilet base and tank:** a wobbling toilet or water at the base of the bowl can indicate a failing wax ring, while a hissing tank can mean an internal leak that's not obvious from the outside.
- **Exterior hose bibs:** check for dripping or dampness on the wall around outdoor faucets, especially after a freeze.

## What to do with what you find

If you spot an active drip or dampness, you've likely found your leak, and depending on the source, it may be a simple fix (a loose supply line fitting) or something requiring more attention (a failing valve or a crack in a pipe). If you've checked all the obvious spots and still see meter movement with no visible signs anywhere, the leak may be underground, in a slab, or inside a wall cavity where it's not accessible without specialized detection equipment.

## Why speed matters here

A leak that seems minor, even one you can't quite locate, can quietly waste hundreds or thousands of gallons of water over weeks and cause mold, warped flooring, or structural damage long before it becomes visually obvious.
