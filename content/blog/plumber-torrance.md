Torrance is one of the largest cities in LA County by area, and its plumbing needs reflect that diversity. From the older neighborhoods near downtown Torrance and the Torrance Beach area to the newer developments in the western and northern parts of the city, Torrance homes span nearly every era of Southern California construction.

## What Torrance homeowners deal with

**Diverse housing stock.** Torrance has homes from every decade since the 1910s. Older homes near the beach and downtown have original galvanized pipes, cast iron drains, and clay sewer lines. Newer developments in the Del Amo area and northern Torrance have modern PEX and PVC systems. Each type of home has its own maintenance needs and failure patterns.

**Beach area corrosion.** Homes near Torrance Beach and the coast experience faster corrosion on exterior plumbing. Outdoor faucets, irrigation systems, and any exposed copper piping need more frequent inspection.

**Hard water.** The South Bay's water supply carries significant mineral content. Water heaters in Torrance homes battle sediment buildup that reduces efficiency and shortens lifespan. Aerators and showerheads get clogged with calcium deposits.

**Sewer line age.** Many Torrance homes built before the 1970s still have clay sewer mains. Tree roots from mature landscaping infiltrate joints and cause blockages. If you're experiencing slow drains across multiple fixtures, the main line is the first place to look.

## Tips for Torrance homeowners

- Flush your water heater annually
- Schedule sewer camera inspections for homes over 40 years old
- Replace outdoor faucet washers and check for corrosion annually if you're near the coast
- Track your water bill — unexplained increases often signal hidden leaks
