A burst pipe or an overflowing toilet doesn't wait for a convenient time. It happens at midnight, on a holiday, or five minutes before you're about to leave for work, and the panic that comes with watching water spread across your floor can make it hard to think clearly. Having a plan you already know, before you ever need it, is what turns those first chaotic minutes into a controlled response instead.

## Step 1: Shut off the water

This is the single most important action in almost any plumbing emergency, because it stops the problem from getting worse while you handle everything else.

- **For a specific fixture** (a toilet, sink, or washing machine), look for a shutoff valve directly behind or below it and turn it clockwise until it stops.
- **For a burst pipe or a leak you can't isolate to one fixture**, go straight to your home's main water shutoff valve, typically located where the water line enters the house, in a basement, crawlspace, utility closet, or near the water meter, and shut it off completely.

If you don't already know where your main shutoff valve is, find it today, not during the next emergency. A valve you have to search for while water is spreading across your floor costs you precious minutes you don't have.

## Step 2: Address electrical safety if water is near outlets or appliances

Water and electricity are a dangerous combination. If flooding is anywhere near electrical outlets, appliances, or a breaker panel, and you can safely reach your home's main breaker without stepping into standing water, shut off power to the affected area. If reaching the breaker means walking through water, don't do it. Leave the area and wait for help instead.

## Step 3: Contain the water

Once the source is shut off, focus on limiting the damage already in progress.

- Use towels, buckets, and a wet/dry shop vac if you have one to remove standing water.
- Move furniture, rugs, and belongings out of the affected area.
- Open windows or run fans to help the area dry out and reduce the chance of mold setting in.

## Step 4: Document the damage

Before you start cleaning everything up, take photos and video of the affected area, the source if you can identify it, and any damaged belongings. This documentation matters if you end up filing an insurance claim, and it's much easier to gather while the damage is fresh than to reconstruct later from memory.

## Step 5: Call a professional for anything beyond a simple fixture

Some plumbing emergencies genuinely are simple once the water is shut off, like a supply line that popped loose and just needs reconnecting. Many others are not, including:

- A burst pipe inside a wall or ceiling
- Sewage backing up into the house
- A water heater leaking or showing signs of a failing pressure relief valve
- Any situation where you can't identify or reach the source of the leak
- Flooding that's already reached electrical systems

For these, shutting off the water and containing the damage is the extent of what you should attempt on your own. Trying to diagnose or repair the actual problem in the middle of an active emergency, especially inside a wall or with gas or electrical systems nearby, risks making the situation worse or putting yourself at risk.
