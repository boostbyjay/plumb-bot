You fill a glass at the kitchen sink and the water looks milky white or hazy, and your first instinct is probably to wonder if something's wrong with it. In the overwhelming majority of cases, this is completely harmless. Here's how to tell the difference between normal cloudiness and something worth paying attention to.

## The Most Common Cause: Trapped Air

Cold water can hold more dissolved air than warm water, and that air gets forced into tiny bubbles whenever water pressure changes suddenly, like when it travels through your pipes and out a faucet aerator. Those microscopic air bubbles scatter light, which is what makes the water look cloudy or milky, exactly the same principle behind why club soda looks different from still water.

## The Simple Glass Test

Here's the easiest way to confirm this is what's happening:

1. Fill a clear glass with tap water
2. Set it on the counter and don't touch it
3. Watch closely for 30 to 60 seconds

If the cloudiness is caused by trapped air, it will clear **from the bottom up**, since the bubbles rise and escape at the surface, leaving clear water behind starting at the base of the glass. This is the telltale sign of harmless air bubbles rather than an actual contaminant, and it's genuinely nothing to worry about.

## When Cloudiness Doesn't Clear Up

If the water stays cloudy, hazy, or discolored well after several minutes, or if it doesn't clear from the bottom up the way trapped air does, that's a different situation worth paying attention to. A few possible causes:

- **Sediment or mineral particles**, especially after nearby water main work or a sudden pressure change in the municipal system, which can stir up material inside pipes
- **A failing water filtration system**, if you have one installed and it's no longer filtering properly
- **Corroding galvanized pipes**, in older homes, which can shed particles into the water over time
- **A municipal supply issue**, particularly if neighbors report the same thing

## What to Do If It Doesn't Clear

If the glass test shows persistent cloudiness rather than air bubbles clearing from the bottom, a few practical steps:

1. Run the cold tap for a minute or two to see if it's a temporary sediment flush after nearby work
2. Check if neighbors are experiencing the same issue, which points toward the municipal supply rather than your home
3. Note whether it's happening at every faucet or just one, which helps narrow down whether it's a whole-house or isolated issue
4. Consider a water test if the cloudiness is persistent and unexplained
