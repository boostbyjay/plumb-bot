Pour baking soda down the drain, follow it with vinegar, watch it fizz dramatically, and your clog is gone. This trick has been shared so many times it's basically plumbing folklore at this point. It looks like a legitimate chemical reaction doing real work, and to be fair, it is a real reaction. The question is whether that reaction is actually strong enough to clear a clog, or whether the satisfying fizz is doing more for your feed than your pipes.

## The chemistry, explained simply

Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, a mild base. Vinegar is acetic acid, a mild acid. When you combine them, they react to produce carbon dioxide gas, which is what creates all that foaming and fizzing. It's the same basic reaction as a volcano science project, and that's not an insult. It's genuinely what's happening in your pipe.

The problem is that this reaction is mild by design. It's not a caustic or corrosive reaction; it's closer to a gentle effervescence. That fizzing action can help loosen light grime, dislodge a thin layer of grease film, or freshen up a drain that smells a little off. What it cannot do is chemically dissolve hair, break down a solid grease clog, or eat through a wad of soap scum the way a stronger acid or base could.

## What it's actually good for

To be clear, this isn't a useless trick. It has legitimate, limited uses:

- Light maintenance cleaning for a drain that's slow but not fully clogged
- Freshening a drain that has a mild odor from minor buildup
- Loosening a thin film of soap scum or light grease residue
- A safe, inexpensive option to try before reaching for anything stronger

## What it won't do

- Clear a hair clog in a bathroom sink or tub drain
- Dissolve a solid grease blockage in a kitchen drain
- Fix a clog that's already causing slow drainage or gurgling
- Reach or affect a blockage that's sitting further down the pipe, past the trap

## Why the "hot water flush" gets the credit

Most versions of this trick end with flushing the drain with hot or boiling water after the fizzing settles down. Here's the part that doesn't get talked about enough: the hot water flush is often doing more of the actual work than the baking soda and vinegar reaction itself. Hot water alone can help move along light grease and soften minor buildup, which is why the combination sometimes seems to "work" even when the fizzing part contributed very little.

## When to skip it and try something else

If a drain is already slow, gurgling, or has backed up even slightly, the baking soda and vinegar trick isn't going to clear it, and running it repeatedly while the real clog gets worse just wastes time. At that point, a plunger or a drain-cleaning tool designed to physically grab and remove hair or debris is a much better next step, since those actually address the blockage instead of hoping a mild chemical reaction will dissolve it.
