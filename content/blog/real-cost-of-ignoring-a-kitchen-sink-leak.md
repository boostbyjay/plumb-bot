A few drops of water under the kitchen sink don't look like an emergency. There's no flooding, nothing dramatic, just a faint dampness on the cabinet floor that you notice, mop up, and mean to look at "this weekend." Weeks later, that weekend still hasn't come, and the leak is quietly doing more damage than its size would suggest.

## Why small leaks are deceptively expensive

A leak that seems minor because it's slow is actually the most dangerous kind, precisely because it's slow. A pipe that bursts gets fixed immediately because you can't ignore standing water on your kitchen floor. A slow drip under a closed cabinet door can continue for months without anyone noticing, because nothing forces the issue the way a flood does.

## What actually happens, month by month

**Weeks 1 to 4: The cabinet floor absorbs moisture.** Most cabinet bases are particleboard or plywood, materials that swell, warp, and delaminate when they stay damp. Even a small, steady drip will eventually saturate the cabinet floor beneath it.

**Month 2: Mold gets a foothold.** Dark, enclosed, and consistently damp, the space under a kitchen sink is close to an ideal environment for mold growth. Once it starts, it spreads along damp surfaces and can be difficult to fully remove without replacing the affected material.

**Months 3 and beyond: The subfloor becomes involved.** If the leak continues long enough, water works its way past the cabinet floor into the subfloor underneath, the structural layer that supports your kitchen flooring. Subfloor rot is a significantly larger repair than a cabinet floor, often requiring flooring removal to access and replace the damaged wood.

**Ongoing: The water bill creeps up.** Even a small drip adds up over time, often unnoticed until the monthly bill jumps and you go looking for why.

## Comparing the cost of acting early vs. waiting

- **Catching it in week one:** Usually means tightening a fitting or replacing a worn washer, often a cost measured in a few dollars for parts and an hour of your time.
- **Catching it after a month:** May involve replacing a section of cabinet flooring in addition to the plumbing fix, a moderate added cost.
- **Catching it after several months:** Can mean mold remediation, cabinet replacement, and subfloor repair on top of the original plumbing issue, a repair bill that's an order of magnitude larger than fixing the leak itself would have been.

The plumbing fix rarely changes much regardless of when you address it. What changes dramatically is everything the water damages while you wait.

## Signs you might already have one

- A faint musty smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
- Discoloration or a chalky white mineral ring on the cabinet floor
- Any give or softness when you press on the cabinet base
- Warped or bulging cabinet material near the corners
- Unexplained increases on your water bill

## The takeaway

The lesson here isn't complicated: a kitchen sink leak is cheapest to fix the day you notice it, and every week you wait adds to both the scope of the repair and its cost. It doesn't take a major leak to cause major damage, it just takes time, which is the one thing a slow drip has plenty of.
