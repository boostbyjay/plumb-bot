Temple City is one of the San Gabriel Valley's most established residential communities. The homes here were built primarily between the 1940s and 1970s, and many families have owned their Temple City homes for generations. That longevity is a testament to the quality of the construction — but it also means the plumbing systems in these homes have been in service for 50, 60, even 70 years.

## Temple City's plumbing landscape

**Aging drain systems.** Many Temple City homes have original cast iron drain stacks or clay sewer lines. Cast iron is durable but develops internal corrosion over time, reducing flow capacity. Clay sewer lines are vulnerable to root intrusion from the area's mature oak and sycamore trees.

**Water heater concerns.** With the San Gabriel Valley's hard water working against them, Temple City water heaters often don't reach their expected lifespan. If your water heater is over 10 years old, start budgeting for replacement before it fails unexpectedly.

**Galvanized supply lines.** Homes built before 1970 may still have sections of galvanized piping. The inside diameter narrows over decades from mineral deposits and corrosion, causing low water pressure and reduced flow at fixtures.

**Drain cleaning needs.** Kitchen drains clog from accumulated grease, bathroom drains collect hair and soap scum, and older pipes with reduced interiors make clogs more frequent. Hydro jetting is particularly effective for Temple City homes with older, narrower pipes.

## Keeping your Temple City home's plumbing healthy

- Flush your water heater annually to remove hard water sediment
- Run hot water and dish soap through kitchen drains after cooking
- Schedule a sewer camera inspection every few years for homes over 40 years old
- Track your water bill month to month — unexplained increases often signal hidden leaks
