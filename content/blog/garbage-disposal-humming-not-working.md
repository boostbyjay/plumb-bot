There's a specific kind of frustration that comes from flipping the disposal switch and hearing a low hum instead of the usual grinding roar. It's not silent, which tells you something is happening electrically, but nothing is actually spinning. This particular symptom has a fairly predictable cause, and it's usually one you can fix yourself in a couple of minutes.

## What the Hum Actually Tells You

A hum with no grinding almost always means the motor is receiving power and trying to turn, but something is physically blocking the flywheel or blades from rotating. If the disposal were truly dead electrically, you'd hear nothing at all. The hum is the motor straining against an obstruction, and if you let it keep trying, it can trip an internal safety switch or, worse, burn out the motor.

## The Two Most Likely Culprits

1. **A jammed flywheel**: something solid, a fruit pit, bone fragment, piece of glass, or utensil, has gotten wedged between the flywheel and the grinding ring, physically stopping rotation.
2. **An overheated motor that's already tripped**: if the disposal has been humming or straining for a while, the built-in thermal overload switch may have already tripped to protect the motor, meaning it needs a manual reset in addition to clearing the jam.

## Step-by-Step: Clearing the Jam

**Tools needed:**

- Disposal wrench or 1/4-inch hex Allen key
- Flashlight
- Tongs or pliers

1. **Turn off power** at the wall switch and, ideally, at the breaker too. This step is not optional given that the motor is actively engaged.
2. **Insert the hex key** into the hole on the bottom center of the disposal unit.
3. **Rotate it firmly in both directions** until you feel the resistance release and the flywheel spins freely.
4. **Shine a flashlight down into the chamber** from the top, with power still off, and remove any visible object with tongs. Never reach in with your hand.
5. **Locate and press the reset button** on the bottom of the unit, a small red or black button, which restores the motor after a thermal trip.
6. **Restore power** and test with the water running.

## If It Hums Again Right Away

If you clear an obvious jam and it still hums without turning, there may be a second object still lodged inside, or the flywheel itself could be damaged. Repeat the manual rotation step and check thoroughly with the flashlight before testing again.

## When the Hum Means Something Electrical

Occasionally, a humming disposal isn't mechanically jammed at all. If the flywheel spins freely with the hex key but the disposal still just hums when powered on, the motor windings themselves may be failing, which is not something to keep testing repeatedly. Continuing to flip the switch on a motor that's electrically failing risks tripping your breaker or, in rare cases, creates a fire hazard.

## The Bottom Line

A humming disposal is one of the more fixable problems in your kitchen, and most of the time a hex key and two minutes of patience solves it.
