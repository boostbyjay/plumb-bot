Covina is one of the San Gabriel Valley's classic family cities — established neighborhoods, good schools, and a strong sense of community. The housing stock is primarily post-war construction from the 1940s through 1970s, and while these homes tend to be well-built, their plumbing wasn't designed to last 70-plus years without maintenance.

## Covina's common plumbing challenges

**Cast iron and galvanized pipes.** Many Covina homes built before 1980 still have cast iron drain lines and sections of galvanized supply piping. Cast iron develops internal corrosion over decades, and galvanized pipes narrow from mineral deposits. Both eventually lead to reduced water pressure, slow drains, and eventual failure.

**Water heater age.** Hard water in the SGV takes a serious toll on water heaters. A water heater that's the same age as your Covina home is likely overdue for replacement. Most water heaters last 8-12 years, and pushing past that means risking a tank failure.

**Sewer line issues.** Clay sewer lines running from the street to the house are common in Covina. Tree roots from mature landscaping infiltrate joints and cause blockages. If you're experiencing slow drains across multiple fixtures, the main line is the first place to check.

**Slab foundation leaks.** Many Covina homes are on concrete slab foundations. Pinhole leaks in copper water lines under the slab can be difficult to detect but cause significant damage over time. Signs include warm floor spots, a running water sound with everything off, and unexplained water bill increases.

## Tips for Covina homeowners

Flush your water heater annually to fight hard water sediment. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every few years for older homes. Track your water bill month to month. And keep a licensed plumber's number on hand before you need one.
