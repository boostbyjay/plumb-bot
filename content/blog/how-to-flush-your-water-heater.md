Most homeowners never think about their water heater until it stops working, but a once-a-year flush is one of the cheapest ways to add years to its life. Minerals and sediment in your water supply settle to the bottom of the tank over time, and left alone, that buildup insulates the water from the heating element or burner, forces the unit to work harder, and eventually causes the popping and rumbling sounds that signal real wear.

## What you'll need

- A garden hose
- A bucket (optional, for the first bit of hot water)
- Flathead screwdriver (for gas units with a pilot control knob)
- Work gloves

## Shutting everything down safely

1. **Turn off power to the unit.** For electric water heaters, switch off the breaker dedicated to the heater. For gas units, turn the gas control valve to "pilot" rather than fully off, so you're not relighting the pilot from scratch afterward. Never drain a tank while it's actively heating, since running it dry with the heat still on can damage the heating element or crack the tank.
2. **Shut off the cold water supply.** Find the cold water inlet valve on top of the tank and turn it off. This stops new water from entering while you drain the old water and sediment out.
3. **Connect a hose to the drain valve.** Attach a standard garden hose to the drain valve near the bottom of the tank and run the other end to a floor drain, outside, or a large bucket, making sure the hose runs downhill the whole way.

## Draining and flushing the tank

4. **Open a hot water tap somewhere in the house**, like an upstairs bathroom sink. This breaks the vacuum inside the tank and lets it drain properly instead of glugging or stalling partway through.
5. **Open the drain valve and let it flow.** The first water out will be hot, so keep hands and skin clear of the hose end. You'll likely see cloudy or sediment-flecked water at first, which is exactly what you're trying to remove.
6. **Flush with a burst of cold water.** Once the tank is empty, briefly open the cold water supply valve for 30 seconds to a minute with the drain valve still open. This stirs up and flushes out any remaining sediment sitting in the bottom of the tank. Repeat once or twice if the water is still cloudy.

## Refilling and restarting

7. **Close the drain valve and disconnect the hose**, then turn the cold water supply back on fully. Leave the hot water tap you opened earlier running until water flows steadily with no air sputtering, which means the tank has refilled completely.
8. **Restore power or relight the pilot.** Turn the breaker back on for electric units, or turn the gas control back to its normal setting and relight the pilot per your unit's instructions if needed.

## Why this matters more than it seems

A tank that's never been flushed can accumulate inches of sediment at the bottom over several years, particularly in areas with hard water. That buildup doesn't just make noise. It reduces the effective capacity of your tank, makes the heating element or burner work overtime, and is one of the most common reasons a water heater fails years earlier than it should.

If your tank hasn't been flushed in a few years, the sediment may be heavy enough that a full flush needs extra care to avoid clogging the drain valve.
