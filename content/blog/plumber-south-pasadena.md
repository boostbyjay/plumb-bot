South Pasadena may be small — just 3.4 square miles — but its plumbing challenges punch well above its weight. With some of the oldest homes in Los Angeles County, a vibrant main street corridor, and a tight-knit community that takes pride in preserving its historic character, South Pasadena homeowners need plumbers who understand the balance between maintaining old systems and upgrading them safely.

## What makes South Pasadena unique

**Histic bungalows and Craftsman homes.** South Pasadena is famous for its Craftsman bungalows, many built between 1905 and 1930. These homes often have original plumbing — galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain stacks, and clay sewer lines that have been in the ground for nearly a century. Working on these homes requires care: you can't just rip out everything and start over. The plumbing needs to be upgraded while preserving the home's character.

**Small lot sizes, mature trees.** South Pasadena's residential lots are compact by modern standards, and the mature trees that give the city its charm have root systems that press right up against sewer lines. Root intrusion is a particularly common problem here.

**Hard water from the SGV supply.** Like the rest of the San Gabriel Valley, South Pasadena deals with hard water that shortens water heater life and leaves scale on fixtures.

**Proximity to Pasadena and LA.** South Pasadena's location between Pasadena and Los Angeles means many residents work in nearby cities and expect quick, professional service close to home.

## Tips for South Pasadena homeowners

- Schedule sewer camera inspections every 2-3 years for homes over 70 years old
- Flush your water heater annually to fight sediment buildup
- When upgrading plumbing, work with a plumber who understands historic home construction
- Don't ignore recurring drain clogs — in South Pasadena, they're often a sewer line issue
