Lakewood is one of LA County's classic post-war suburbs — the kind of neighborhood where families have lived for generations and where the houses were built to last. But "built to last" doesn't mean "built to last forever without maintenance," and Lakewood's plumbing systems are starting to show their age.

## Lakewood's plumbing reality

**Post-war construction.** Most Lakewood homes were built in the 1950s and early 1960s. Original galvanized supply piping, cast iron drain stacks, and clay sewer lines are the norm. Galvanized pipes corrode from the inside over 60+ years, and many Lakewood homes are dealing with reduced water pressure as a result.

**Water heater replacements.** If your Lakewood home still has its original water heater, it's been running on borrowed time for years. Water heaters have a 10-12 year lifespan, and pushing past that means risking a tank failure that can flood your garage or utility room in minutes.

**Sewer line age.** Clay sewer mains running from the street to your house have been in the ground since the home was built. Tree roots from mature neighborhood trees infiltrate joints and cause blockages. If multiple drains are draining slowly at once, the main line is almost certainly the issue.

**Hard water across LA County.** Lakewood shares the regional hard water supply. Sediment in water heaters, scale on fixtures, and reduced appliance efficiency are ongoing concerns.

## Lakewood maintenance tips

- Flush your water heater annually
- Schedule a sewer camera inspection every few years for homes over 40 years old
- Track your water bill month to month
- Know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it
