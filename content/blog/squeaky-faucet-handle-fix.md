Every faucet handle has a personality, and some of them announce themselves with a little squeak every single time you turn them. It's easy to tune out, the kind of sound you stop noticing after a while, the same way you stop hearing a ticking clock. But that squeak is actually your faucet telling you something, and it's worth a two-minute fix before it turns into a much bigger annoyance.

## What's actually causing the squeak

Inside most faucet handles, there's a metal stem that threads in and out of the faucet body every time you turn the water on and off, or in the case of single-handle faucets, a cartridge that pivots and rotates against internal surfaces. Both designs rely on smooth metal-on-metal or metal-on-plastic contact. Over months and years of use, the factory lubrication that was applied when the faucet was manufactured wears away, and you're left with dry parts rubbing against each other. That friction is what you're hearing.

## Why this is worth fixing, not just ignoring

A squeaky handle on its own isn't dangerous or urgent. But left alone, that friction gradually increases. What starts as an annoying sound can progress into a handle that gets stiffer and harder to turn, and eventually one that seizes up entirely, at which point you're forcing it, risking a stripped stem or a cracked handle, usually at the worst possible moment.

## The fix: plumber's grease on the stem threads

This is the overlooked part. Most homeowners either ignore the squeak indefinitely or, worse, reach for a general household lubricant like a petroleum-based spray, which isn't rated for contact with drinking water and can actually degrade rubber washers and O-rings over time. What you actually want is plumber's grease, sometimes labeled as silicone faucet grease, available at any hardware store for just a few dollars.

## What you'll need

- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Adjustable wrench or Allen key (depending on your handle type)
- Screwdriver
- A clean rag

## How to do it

1. Shut off the water supply under the sink and open the faucet to release pressure.
2. Remove the handle by taking off the decorative cap (if there is one), removing the screw underneath, and lifting the handle straight up.
3. Loosen and remove the retaining nut to expose the stem or cartridge.
4. Wipe the stem threads clean with a rag to remove any old, dried-out lubricant or grit.
5. Apply a thin, even coat of plumber's grease to the threads and any O-rings you can see.
6. Reassemble everything in reverse order, turn the water back on, and test the handle. The squeak should be gone, and the turn should feel noticeably smoother.

## A good habit, not just a one-time fix

Since this friction naturally returns over time, it's worth doing this same quick lubrication anytime you're already inside the faucet for another reason, like replacing a washer or cartridge. Think of it the same way you'd think about oiling a squeaky door hinge: a small maintenance habit that prevents a bigger problem later.

## When it's more than dry threads

If you've regreased the stem and the handle still squeaks, or if it's stiff to turn even after lubrication, the internal parts themselves may be worn out or corroded rather than just dry. That's usually the point where a cartridge or stem replacement makes more sense than another round of grease.
