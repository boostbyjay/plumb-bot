Homes built on a concrete slab foundation run water and sometimes sewer lines directly beneath that slab, which works fine for decades until one of those pipes starts to leak. A slab leak is one of the more unnerving plumbing problems a homeowner can face, mainly because it's completely out of sight until the damage has already been building for a while.

## Warning sign 1: Warm spots on the floor

If you have a hot water line running under your slab and it develops a leak, you may actually feel a noticeably warm patch on your flooring, even through carpet or tile, in an area with no other heat source nearby. This is one of the more distinctive signs of a slab leak specifically, since it points directly to a hot water line issue rather than a cold water or drain line.

## Warning sign 2: Unexplained water bill spikes

If your water usage habits haven't changed but your bill has jumped noticeably, a slab leak is one of the possibilities worth investigating, especially if you've already ruled out running toilets and irrigation issues. Because slab leaks are continuous rather than intermittent, they tend to show up as a sustained increase rather than a one-time spike.

## Warning sign 3: The sound of running water when everything is off

Some homeowners notice a faint hissing or running water sound, sometimes audible through the floor itself, even when every fixture in the house is off. This can be subtle enough to dismiss at first, but it's worth taking seriously, especially paired with any of the other signs on this list.

## Warning sign 4: Cracks in flooring or baseboards separating from walls

Water escaping under a slab can erode the soil beneath it or create hydrostatic pressure that shifts the slab slightly. This can show up as new cracks in tile or flooring, doors that suddenly stick, or baseboards pulling away from walls in ways that look more like foundation movement than typical settling.

## The simple confirmation test

The water meter test used for general leak detection also applies here and is a useful first step. With all water off in the house, check whether your meter's leak indicator is moving, or whether the reading changes over a 30 to 60 minute period with nothing running. A positive result confirms a leak somewhere in the system, though it takes further investigation, usually with specialized listening or thermal imaging equipment, to confirm it's specifically under the slab rather than somewhere else.

## Why this isn't a DIY repair

Slab leaks are fundamentally different from a leak under a sink or behind a washing machine. The pipe is embedded in or under concrete, which means locating the exact point of failure requires specialized acoustic or thermal detection equipment, and the repair itself typically involves either cutting into the slab to access and repair the specific section, or rerouting the water line entirely above the slab to bypass the damaged section. Both approaches require real expertise, and getting the location wrong means unnecessary demolition.
